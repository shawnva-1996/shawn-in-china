# Reference Vlogs: How Viral China Travel Videos Are Made

**Purpose:** a working reference for the first Shawn in China video (Chongqing) and the channel after it.
**Compiled:** 21 Oct 2026, using vidIQ data (YouTube search, outlier scores, keyword volumes, transcripts, thumbnail similarity) plus the web sources linked at the end.
**How to read the numbers:** view counts are as of 21 Oct 2026. The *breakout* score is vidIQ's measure of how far a video beat its own channel's typical views (for example, 225x means 225 times the channel's normal result). Breakout is the more useful signal for a new channel, because it shows what works without a big audience behind it.

## 2. Anatomy: how these videos are built

### 2.1 The hook (first 30 to 60 seconds)

Three hook patterns repeat across the set:

1. **Mystery and illusion (Around The World We Go).** It opens on drone footage with narration: *"From high above, Chongqing looks like any other massive city. But step closer, and that illusion starts falling apart one layer at a time… a place where directions are meaningless, where ground level is more of a funny notion than a fact."* It promises a feeling, not a list.
2. **Scale shock plus a personal reason (Drew Binsky).** *"There's a city in China with 36 million people and it's not Shanghai or Beijing."* Then a personal trigger: an Instagram poll showed 58% of his audience had never heard of Chongqing, *"so I bought a ticket."* (We read the Vietnamese-dubbed transcript, which is the default caption track. The structure is clear either way.)
3. **Promise of usefulness (The Travel Intern).** *"If this is your first time heading to Chongqing, stick around because here are some places and activities we recommend."* An app-essentials tip comes before the first location.

**Pattern:** state the paradox ("biggest city you've never heard of", "ground floor is the 22nd floor"), then a reason to stay. The best openers show the payoff shot inside the first 10 seconds and explain it later.

### 2.2 Structure

- **Goal-driven quest (Drew Binsky):** he declares three goals at the start: meet locals who speak English, find the city's most famous hotpot, and do it all for under USD 250. Each goal is a chapter and the ending recaps all three. This gives a loose vlog a spine and a reason to keep watching.
- **Running thread with a payoff (Around The World We Go):** the hunt for a hidden WWII-bunker hotpot restaurant fails twice (*"right bunker, wrong time"*) and succeeds at the end. The repeated failure is the retention device.
- **Chapters as a checklist (Luisito Comunica, CoolVision):** 20 to 30 timestamped chapters, each one recognisable moment (train through building, Hongyadong, Raffles City, hotpot, Ciqikou). Viewers skim and search engines index each chapter.
- **List format (The Travel Intern):** 5 minutes of location after location. It is useful, but has no character arc and gets a modest breakout (7x).

**For us:** a quest spine ("Can a Singaporean go from tourist to Chongqing insider in 6 days?" or "the questions everyone asked me before I went") with chapters for search, and one running thread that pays off at the end.

### 2.3 How context reaches an international viewer

- **One number per scene, said out loud:** "32 million people… roughly the size of Austria", "bombed over 200 times… it never surrendered", "171 RMB, that's 23 dollars for all of this." Concrete numbers stand in for long explanation.
- **History in one or two sentences, over B-roll:** Shancheng Alley *"connecting the upper and lower city since the Ming dynasty"*. Ciqikou *"the porcelain port"*. Context arrives as narration over moving pictures, never as a lecture to camera.
- **A local voice:** Drew uses a local guide (Joy) to explain why people eat spicy food and how cheap rent is. DW uses a city guide and a designated driver. Locals bring authority the host doesn't have.
- **Price reveals:** food bills and rent in USD land well with an international audience.
- **Practical tips built into the story:** "come at 8 to 8:30pm, the crowd has dispersed" at Hongyadong (The Travel Intern). Payment apps are introduced up front.

### 2.4 Pacing, picture and sound

- Long-form winners run **20 to 40 minutes**. The Singapore guide at 5 minutes underperforms its peers. For a first video, **12 to 18 minutes** is a sensible middle: long enough for watch time, short enough to finish.
- Cinematic B-roll (drone, timelapse, night neon) runs under the narration, with on-camera pieces to break it up. Around The World We Go is about 70% voiceover over B-roll, which suits Shawn's footage and his own recorded narration.
- Music changes with each segment (ambient for fog, upbeat for food), and the music drops out for punchlines and reveals.
- Natural sound is kept for moments with real reactions (spice, heights, a surprise).
- The ending is reflective, not a list: *"Chongqing isn't a puzzle. It's not a checklist… it finally clicks when you stop trying so damn hard."* That line makes people share the video.

## 3. Titles: what gets clicked

### 3.1 Formulas seen in the set

| Formula | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Superlative plus "never heard of"** | "The World's Biggest City You've Never Heard Of" | Curiosity gap plus scale |
| **Disbelief** | "Chongqing Is Not Real", "We Had No Idea This City Existed" | Challenges the viewer's map of the world |
| **Confusion** | "World's most confusing city!", "Will Confuse Your Brain (8D Chongqing)" | Promises a visual puzzle |
| **First-person transformation** | "I Thought China Only Copied Luxury. Then I Went There..." | A belief, then a reversal |
| **Professional plus fear or awe** | "I Run a US Factory. What I Just Saw in China Should Terrify You." | Authority plus stakes |
| **Warning or prerequisite** | "Don't Go to Chongqing in 2026 Without Watching This" | Loss aversion, aimed at planners |
| **Time-boxed challenge** | "I Spent 48 Hours in the WORLD'S Most Unreal City" | Clear scope and pace |

Common mechanics: 40 to 70 characters; the keyword "Chongqing" or "China" either up front or in brackets at the end; one or two capitalised words at most; a flag emoji is common but optional.

## 4. Thumbnails: what the evidence says

### 4.1 Patterns in the Chongqing set

- **Spectacle first:** neon skyline, stacked roads, a train in a building or Hongyadong at night take up most of the frame. The city is the star.
- **One face, small to medium, with a clear emotion** (shock, awe) in one corner. Several top videos (Drew Binsky, Abhishek Bishnoi) use the host's face. Others use none.
- **Two to four words of text at most**, often answering or contradicting the title ("NOT REAL?", "8D CITY", "22ND FLOOR = GROUND").
- **High contrast:** a cool night palette (cyan and magenta neon) with warm or yellow text. Arrows and circles are used to point at the impossible detail.
- vidIQ's similar-thumbnail search for "surprised traveller in front of neon Chongqing skyline" returned mostly small and mid channels with modest breakouts (1x–5x). **This concept is common, so on its own it will not stand out.**

### 4.2 Data on faces and testing

- A 1of10 Media analysis of 300,000+ outlier videos (reported by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/)) found **thumbnails with and without faces perform about the same overall**. Faces helped mainly on larger channels, and **multiple faces beat a single face**. Business content did *worse* with faces.
- YouTube's **Test & Compare** (up to 3 thumbnails and titles per video) picks winners by **watch time, not click-through rate**. A clickbait thumbnail that the video doesn't deliver on loses the test.
- vidIQ's own guide: [YouTube Thumbnail Design Tips](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/).

### 4.3 Rules for Shawn in China thumbnails

1. **The impossible detail plus Shawn's reaction.** Use a real frame from the footage, not a stock look. Shawn's face is optional, so test with and without.
2. **Three words maximum**, and they must add to the title, never repeat it.
3. **Make the clickbait true.** Whatever the thumbnail promises appears in the first 60 seconds, which protects watch time in Test & Compare.
4. **Make the differentiator visible.** A robot, a lab or a delegation badge in one corner tells viewers "this is not another tourist vlog".
5. **Upload 3 variants to Test & Compare** on day one.

## 5. Where Shawn in China can win

1. **Insider access is the gap.** Every Chongqing vlog in the set is a tourist's view. Shawn went inside a robotics company, a medical-tech firm, CVTE, a data-centre region and a government trade conference, as a Singaporean on an official study mission. The adjacent business and tech videos break out at 36x to 900x.
2. **The Singapore angle is barely served.** The only Singapore creator in the set made a 5-minute list. Singaporeans can visit visa-free for 30 days and can fund learning trips through SkillsFuture. Nobody is telling that story.
3. **Combine the spectacle with the substance:** open with the 8D and cyberpunk visuals the algorithm already rewards, then pay it off with "and here is what I saw inside that tourists never see".
4. **Use the FAQ structure:** answering the questions first-time visitors search (payments, visas, getting around, spice, safety) gives each chapter search value and positions Shawn as the expert.
5. **Make the Ngee Ann Polytechnic call to action organic:** "how I got inside" is the natural setup for "the programme I went with".

## 6. Notes for the Chinese version (Xiaohongshu and WeChat Channels)

These are platform conventions from general practice, not measured in this study. Validate them on the first posts.

- Vertical 9:16, 45 to 90 seconds per note, one question per video, with the hook in the first 3 seconds.
- A cover image with large Chinese text (4 to 8 characters) on a strong photo. Xiaohongshu users browse a grid of covers, so the cover does the work of a thumbnail.
- Captions in Simplified Chinese, burned in.
- An angle that fits a Chinese audience: "what a Singaporean noticed about Chongqing" and Singapore–Chongqing cooperation (the China–Singapore Connectivity Initiative), rather than "is China real?".
- Hashtags: 重庆旅游, 重庆攻略, 新加坡, 中新互联互通, 8D魔幻城市.
