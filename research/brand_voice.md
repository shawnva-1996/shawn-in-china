# Shawn in China: Brand Voice (v1)

> **Status (22 Oct 2026):** there are no journal images, so this voice is built from the reference study ([reference_vlogs.md](./reference_vlogs.md)) and the shape of the trip itself. It is a starting point. After Shawn records the first narration, tune the "Signature habits" below to how he actually talks, and keep what sounds like him.

## Channel promise

**Chongqing, and China, from the inside, explained by a Singaporean who went to learn and not just to look.**
Every video gives viewers the spectacle they came for, plus the access and answers they can't get elsewhere.

## Who Shawn is on camera

- **The friend who just got back:** practical, specific and a little amazed. He answers the questions you were going to ask anyway.
- **A curious professional, not a tour guide:** he notices how things work (payments, transit, factories, elder care) as well as how they look.
- **A Singaporean lens:** he compares things to home (MRT, hawker prices, HDB) to make them relatable for Singapore viewers and specific for everyone else.

## Voice principles (from the reference study)

1. **Open with a paradox, close with a feeling.** Start on the impossible thing ("the ground floor is on the 22nd floor"). End on what it meant, never on a list.
2. **One number per scene.** Concrete figures (price, height, population) instead of adjectives.
3. **Two sentences of context, then back to the moment.** History and policy arrive as narration over B-roll, never as a lecture.
4. **Honest about friction.** Getting lost, spice pain and payment hiccups are trust-builders, not flaws.
5. **Earn the clickbait.** Whatever the title and thumbnail promise shows up in the first minute.

## Signature habits (working defaults)

These replace the journal-based section. Each one is a default to keep or drop after the first recording.

- **Rhythm:** short spoken sentences, mostly under 15 words. One long sentence per scene at most, usually for context. Read aloud, every line should fit one breath.
- **Openers he reaches for:** "Here's the thing.", "Quick context.", "Okay, honest answer:", "Nobody tells you this, but…". Use each one sparingly, no more than twice per video.
- **What he notices first:** how things *work* (payments, transit, who runs a project), then how they look. The study-mission lens is the point of difference, so the "how it works" beat comes before the "wow" beat in any scene that has both.
- **How he reflects:** a comparison to Singapore, then one honest feeling. For example: "Our MRT goes under buildings. Theirs goes through them. I laughed out loud."
- **Local expressions:** light and natural. An occasional "lah" or "shiok" is fine where it fits the Singapore audience, but never more than a couple per video, and none in lines meant for the international cut's hook.
- **FAQ framing:** questions are said out loud in the viewer's words ("Can you use your card?"), then answered in one or two lines, then he moves on. No question gets more than about 20 seconds.
- **Self-deprecating, never cynical:** he can be the butt of the joke (spice, stairs, getting lost). China and the hosts are not.

## Recording the narration (for Shawn)

- Record in a quiet, soft-furnished room, phone 20–30 cm from your mouth, airplane mode on. Record each section as its own file (`VO_01_cold_open.m4a` and so on).
- Read each line twice: once as written, once in your own words. The edit will use whichever sounds more like you.
- Smile on the hook and the call to action. Slow down for numbers and names.
- If a line marked **[Shawn to confirm]** isn't true for you, say what actually happened instead. Real beats invented every time.

## Do and don't (first pass)

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "I paid 18 RMB for this, about S$3.40." | "It was super cheap!" |
| "This street is on the 22nd floor. I didn't climb a single stair." | "The architecture is mind-blowing." |
| "We weren't allowed to film inside, so here's what they told us." | Skip a stop silently |
| "The programme I went with is NP's Travel & Learn. Link below." | A hard sell or quoted fees |

## Mandarin voice (for Xiaohongshu and WeChat)

- A free stock neural voice, warm male or neutral (to be picked from samples).
- Register: 轻松、真诚、有干货 (relaxed, sincere, substantive). Phrase it as **a Singaporean friend sharing what he learned**, not as a foreigner marvelling at China.
- Each clip opens with the question it answers (e.g. 第一次去重庆，怎么付款？), then gives the answer and a single takeaway.
