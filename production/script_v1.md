## 2. Day 1: Before you go (0:50–2:40)

**Shots:** 12 Oct arrival photos → `IMG_8059.MOV` (round table, tea pour) → `IMG_8070.MOV` (lazy Susan pan) → `IMG_8089.MOV` (beef dishes) → `IMG_8074.MOV` (welcome speech) → `IMG_8092.MOV` (street-food neon) → `IMG_8094.MOV` (tangyuan dessert).

> **VO:** First question everyone asked me: do you need a visa? If you're Singaporean, no. Since February 2024, Singapore and China let each other's citizens visit visa-free for up to 30 days.
>
> Not Singaporean? Check the 240-hour visa-free transit. Chongqing's airport is one of the eligible entry points, as long as you're flying on to a third country. The rules change, so check the official list before you book.

**Card:** 🇸🇬 30 days visa-free (since 9 Feb 2024) · 240-hour transit: check eligibility

> **VO:** Second question: how do you pay? Here's the thing. China barely uses cash or cards in shops. It runs on Alipay and WeChat Pay, and both now accept foreign Visa and Mastercard. Set them up and verify them *before* you fly. I used **[Shawn to confirm: Alipay, WeChat Pay or both]**, and it worked almost everywhere. Still carry a bit of cash for backup.
>
> Third: Google, WhatsApp and Instagram don't work on Chinese networks. **[Shawn to confirm your setup, e.g. a roaming eSIM]** Download offline maps and a translation app before you land. Trust me on the translation app.
>
> Then: welcome dinner. Round table, lazy Susan, and a dish every thirty seconds. I didn't know half of what I was eating. I finished all of it.

## 3. Day 2: The 8D city, and why Singapore is everywhere (2:40–4:40)

**Shots:** 13 Oct `IMG_8126.MOV` (panoramic city model screen) → `IMG_8134.MOV` / `IMG_8137.MOV` (immersive starry projection) → `IMG_8247.MOV` (Admin Bureau tilt-down) → `IMG_8249.MOV` (delegation walking) → `IMG_8320.MOV` / `IMG_8321.MOV` (looking up at linked towers) → Raffles Hospital photos (16:37–17:33) → `IMG_8397.MOV` / `IMG_8398.MOV` (Guanyinqiao LED street).

> **VO:** Quick context, because Chongqing confuses everyone. Why do people call it the 8D city?
>
> It's built on mountains where two rivers meet, the Yangtze and the Jialing. There's barely any flat ground, so the city stacks on itself. Roads run over roofs. You walk into a mall on the ground floor and walk out on the eighth. Your map app has no idea which level you're on.
>
> We started at the Planning Exhibition Gallery, which is basically the city explaining itself. The whole municipality has around 32 million people and is about the size of Austria. Most of that is countryside, but the core still feels endless.
>
> Then a briefing I didn't expect to find so interesting: the China–Singapore Chongqing Connectivity Initiative. It's a government-to-government project, Singapore's third in China after Suzhou and Tianjin. Finance, logistics, aviation, digital links. It's why a Singaporean study group gets a seat at these tables at all.
>
> Even the hospital we visited that afternoon was Singaporean: Raffles Hospital, in the middle of Chongqing. **[Shawn to confirm one detail from the visit]**
>
> And at night, Guanyinqiao. Screens on every building, and the whole street out walking.

**Card:** 8D = mountains + two rivers + no flat ground

## 5. Day 4: Old factories, Singapore on the wall, and Hongyadong at night (7:20–9:40)

**Shots:** 15 Oct `IMG_8624.MOV` (PS5 mural) → `IMG_8635.MOV` (street) → Eling clips (09:17–10:11) → `IMG_8679.MOV` (viewpoint, red arch bridge) → `IMG_8684.MOV` (Connectivity Hall sign) → `IMG_8687.MOV` (leaders on display wall) → `IMG_8690.MOV` (timeline 2015–2025) → `IMG_8697.MOV` (BEYOND 2026 stage) → night: `IMG_8704.MOV` (skyline and red bridge) → `IMG_8705.MOV` → `IMG_8714.MOV` (Hongyadong wide) → `IMG_8722.MOV` / `IMG_8724.MOV` (vertical stilt houses) → `IMG_8723.MOV` (street singer) → `IMG_8739.MOV` (neon cruise boat) → `IMG_8741.MOV` (high angle).

> **VO:** If you think Chongqing is only neon, start here. Eling No. 2 Factory used to print money for China's central bank in the 1940s. Now it's murals, cafés and studios. Yes, that's a PlayStation controller on a wall.
>
> In the afternoon, the Connectivity Initiative exhibition hall. Seeing Singapore's leaders on the wall in Chongqing hit differently. **[Shawn to confirm]** Then a trade conference, Singapore and Chongqing businesses trying to find each other.
>
> And then the night everyone comes for. Hongyadong.
>
> It's a cliffside complex of stilt houses, lit gold every night, and people say it looks like *Spirited Away*. When should you go? After dark. The usual advice is to skip the 7:30 crush and aim for after eight. We got there just after eight. Busy, but worth it.
>
> Is it safe at night? From everything I saw, yes. Families out late, people everywhere, just mind your phone in the crowds like anywhere.
>
> And stand on the river side and look back. That's the 8D city in one view.

**Card:** Hongyadong tip: go after 8pm · watch from the river side

> **VO:** Day five was the nerdy day, and I mean that as a compliment.
>
> Ever heard of "East Data, West Computing"? China's richest cities are on the east coast, and so is most of the demand for computing. But land and power are cheaper out west. So the country is building huge data centres in places like Chongqing to do the computing for the east. We visited one. No filming inside, so here's the group photo.
