# Day 2 — Liangjiang Life Technologies City (两江生命科技城)

**Visit:** Day 2 of Shawn's China trip.  
**Source:** User-pasted OCR from a bilingual Chinese/English promotional brochure about Liangjiang Life Technologies City and Dadi Life Science and Technology Park. The OCR has duplication, mistranslations, garbled company names and numbers. All statistics and technology claims below are brochure claims unless explicitly described as a planning target.

## The short version

Liangjiang Life Technologies City is Chongqing's planned hub for life sciences and healthcare businesses. It wants pharmaceutical companies, medical-device makers, laboratories, research teams, investors and specialist service providers to work near one another.

**In layman English:** Imagine a business park built specifically for healthcare innovation. A company developing a new medical device might need engineers, a lab, safety testing, regulatory advice, funding, a factory and hospital partners. The park is trying to put many of those resources within one connected ecosystem so that ideas can move from research to testing, production and sale more easily.

Dadi Life Science and Technology Park (大地生命科技园) is presented as one important part of that wider plan, in the Shuitu New City area of Liangjiang New Area.

## What the park is trying to build

The brochure groups the core industries into a “3+3” system.

### Three main industry clusters

1. **Medical devices:** patient monitors, diagnostic instruments, surgical tools and robots, rehabilitation equipment, imaging systems, orthopaedic products and life-support equipment.
2. **Biological medicines:** medicines made using biological processes, including antibodies, biosimilars, recombinant proteins, vaccines, and cell and gene therapies.
3. **Chemical medicines:** conventional small-molecule drugs, new medicines, improved formulations and quality generic medicines.

### Three emerging areas

- **Modernised traditional Chinese medicine:** new or improved products based on Chinese-medicine ingredients and techniques.
- **Specialist services:** companies that help other firms research, test, develop and manufacture medicines or devices.
- **Digital and new healthcare:** telemedicine, medical AI and healthcare data.

The wider Chongqing health strategy also includes medical services, pharmaceuticals, health and elder care, fitness/wellness and health management.

## The three themed parks

- **D-Park / 百药谷 (“medicine valley”):** focused on biological medicines and drug development. Brochure plan: about 3,700 mu; 2035 output target around RMB 26 billion.
- **M-Park / 千械港 (“medical-device harbour”):** focused on medical devices and related innovation. Brochure plan: about 4,200 mu; 2035 output target around RMB 40 billion.
- **3C-Park / 3C岛:** intended for company headquarters, industry services and innovation platforms. The brochure lists about 181 mu.

The broader strategy also mentions Mingyue Lake Life Science and Technology Innovation Park and Zhaomushan Digital Healthcare Park as complementary R&D and digital-health locations.

## Dadi Life Science and Technology Park: figures quoted in the brochure

The OCR reports:
- Planned site: about **650 mu** (approximately 43 hectares).
- Planned total floor area: about **1 million m²**.
- Long-term capacity: **250+ companies**.
- Built at the time represented by the brochure: about **700,000 m²**.
- Companies already in the park at that time: about **170** — more than 130 in Dadi Enterprise Park and more than 30 in Dadi (International) Life Science Park.
- Approximate company mix: **60% medical devices, 30% biopharmaceuticals and 10% inspection/testing**.
- The OCR mentions 20+ larger-scale enterprises, 50+ high-tech enterprises and 80+ innovative pharmaceutical/technology enterprises in one passage; a duplicated English passage says “more than 60” for one category, so the exact count is inconsistent.
- Hundreds of products are said to have NMPA, FDA or CE certifications.
- The brochure says it hopes to cultivate 10+ companies for stock-market listing within five years.
- Full-buildout targets are quoted as roughly **RMB 30 billion output**, **RMB 3 billion tax revenue** and **15,000 jobs**.

These figures may refer to different years, definitions or projections. They are not verified current figures.

## The wider Liangjiang plan and targets

The brochure says that in **2024** the life-and-health sector in Liangjiang New Area had a total industry scale of **RMB 13.11 billion**, industrial output from above-designated-size firms of **RMB 8.876 billion**, and nearly **300 related companies**. It says the industrial output was around 10% of Chongqing's citywide total.

Brochure targets:
- **2025:** exceed RMB 50 billion in industry scale and establish a distinctive life-and-health cluster in western China.
- **2035:** exceed RMB 100 billion, with 100+ pharmaceutical companies and 1,000+ medical-device companies.

These are plans and targets, not proof that the targets were achieved. The brochure also gives separate targets for the themed parks: D-Park RMB 26 billion, M-Park RMB 40 billion and a formulation-centre area RMB 20 billion by 2035.

## How the park says it will help companies

The brochure's model combines four things:
- **Industrial park:** workspace, buildings and facilities.
- **Innovation incubator:** support for early-stage companies and new projects.
- **Industrial fund:** access to investment and financing.
- **Industry alliance:** connections to other companies, experts, suppliers and customers.

It also lists shared or specialist platforms for medical-device testing, certification, engineering, biomedical research, clinical/medical translation, cold-chain logistics, bioanalysis, antibody development and contract manufacturing.

In plain English, the park is not just selling office or factory space. It is selling access to a network of facilities, specialist expertise, funding and potential business partners. The practical value depends on which services are actually operating, their cost, and whether a company can access them.

## Companies and technologies highlighted

The brochure profiles or names many organisations. Examples include:

- **Haifu Medical (海扶医疗):** focused-ultrasound technology intended to treat certain tumours without conventional open surgery.
- **SWS / 山外山:** blood-purification and dialysis equipment.
- **BORN / 博恩富克:** non-invasive brain monitoring; the brochure discusses brain-oedema and haematoma monitoring.
- **BOSSCOME / 博仕康:** surgical navigation systems for precision and minimally invasive procedures.
- **Porton Pharma Solutions / 博腾:** contract development and manufacturing for drug companies (**CDMO**).
- **Boji Medical / 博济医药:** outsourced drug/device research and development services (**CRO/CDMO**).
- **Pharmaron / 康龙化成** and **JOINN / 昭衍新药:** research and development services for drug developers.
- **Evaheart / 永仁心:** implantable artificial-heart/ventricular-assist technology.
- **MakelD / 品胜科技:** medical wristbands, label printers and digital identification/asset-management systems.
- **UTECH / 如泰科技:** digital medical devices and patient-monitoring products.
- Other named firms include Linghang Medical, Impel Medical, Wistron Medical, Huapont Pharma, Yaopharma and PKU Healthcare, alongside diagnostic and medical-device companies.

These are examples shown in the brochure, not a verified list of current tenants. Claims about being “first,” “largest,” “world-leading,” patent totals, regulatory approvals or clinical outcomes should be checked independently.

## Two useful industry terms

- **CRO — Contract Research Organisation:** a company hired to carry out research or trials for another company.
- **CDMO — Contract Development and Manufacturing Organisation:** a company hired to help develop a product and manufacture it, rather than the drug owner building all the capability itself.

These service providers matter because a small biotech firm may not be able to afford its own laboratories, specialist staff and production plant.

## Why this may matter commercially

The business opportunities are not limited to inventing a new drug. The ecosystem also needs testing and certification, medical-device engineering, quality and regulatory support, outsourced research and manufacturing, clinical research, laboratory equipment, cold-chain logistics, healthcare software and AI, financing, and help commercialising products overseas.

For a technology or services company, the useful question is: **Which of these needs are local companies struggling to solve, and can the park provide access to paying customers, pilot projects, partners or incentives?**

## Questions to ask before relying on the brochure

1. Which named companies are physically operating there today, rather than simply featured in promotional material?
2. Which shared platforms are operational, what do they cost, and can outside SMEs use them?
3. What grants, tax incentives, rent support or R&D funding are available now, and what are the eligibility conditions?
4. Which hospitals and research institutions can support pilots or clinical validation?
5. Who helps with China's NMPA and overseas FDA/CE requirements?
6. What year does each occupancy, revenue, employment or output number describe?
7. What conditions apply to foreign companies, intellectual property, data, local partnerships and taking profits overseas?

## Bottom line

Liangjiang is trying to create an end-to-end healthcare innovation cluster: research, testing, funding, manufacturing and commercialisation in one connected region. Dadi Park is presented as an existing base within that ambition. The brochure shows the ecosystem's intended scale and examples of its companies, but promotional claims and targets should not be confused with independently verified current performance.
