# Day 2 — Liangjiang Life Technologies City (两江生命科技城)

**Visit date:** Day 2 of Shawn's China trip; exact calendar date not supplied in the source OCR.  
**Source:** User-provided OCR from a bilingual Chinese/English industrial-park brochure. OCR contains duplicated passages, mistranslations and recognition errors. Figures below are brochure claims, not independently verified current statistics.

## Executive summary

Liangjiang Life Technologies City is Chongqing Liangjiang New Area's planned life-sciences and healthcare industry hub. The pitch is to cluster pharmaceutical companies, medical-device manufacturers, research institutions, testing/certification services, investors and clinical partners so that a medical idea can move from R&D to testing, manufacturing and commercial sale.

Dadi Life Science and Technology Park (大地生命科技园) is presented as an established component of this wider development, located in the Shuitu New City area. The brochure describes an integrated ecosystem of laboratories, incubators, industrial funds, industry alliances, manufacturing facilities and service platforms.

## In plain English

Think of it as a purpose-built neighbourhood for the healthcare industry. Instead of a biotech start-up having to find a lab, specialist equipment, regulatory testing, manufacturing partners, funding and hospital contacts separately, the park wants those resources close together. The local government hopes this makes it easier to start companies, develop products, attract investment and create skilled jobs.

## The main industry areas

The brochure's “3+3” system has three core clusters:
1. **Medical devices** — diagnostic instruments, surgical tools and robots, rehabilitation equipment, imaging, orthopaedic products, life-support equipment and digital health electronics.
2. **Biological products** — biologic medicines, biosimilars, antibodies, recombinant proteins, vaccines, and cell/gene therapies.
3. **Chemical medicines** — conventional and innovative small-molecule drugs, improved formulations and high-quality generic medicines.

It also highlights three emerging areas:
- **New traditional Chinese medicine** and modernised Chinese-medicine products.
- **New services** such as drug/device CRO and CDMO services.
- **New medical care**, including telemedicine, medical AI and healthcare data.

The wider health strategy also mentions medical services, pharmaceuticals, health and elder care, fitness/wellness and health management.

## Three themed parks

- **D-Park / 百药谷 (“medicine valley”)**: biological products and drug-formulation development. The brochure gives a planned area of about 3,700 mu, with a 2035 output target of RMB 26 billion.
- **M-Park / 千械港 (“thousand-device harbour”)**: medical devices and related innovation/service platforms. Planned area about 4,200 mu; 2035 output target RMB 40 billion.
- **3C-Park / 3C岛**: headquarters, industry-service organisations and innovation platforms. The brochure lists about 181 mu.

The wider city is also described as working with Mingyue Lake Life Science and Technology Innovation Park and Zhaomushan Digital Healthcare Park. These are presented as complementary R&D/translation and digital-health areas.

## Dadi Life Science and Technology Park — brochure figures

The OCR reports:
- Total planned land: about **650 mu** (roughly 43 hectares).
- Planned total floor area: about **1 million m²**.
- Planned capacity: **250+ companies**.
- At the time represented by the brochure: about **700,000 m² built**, with around **170 companies**.
- Company mix: roughly **60% medical devices, 30% biopharmaceuticals, 10% inspection/testing**.
- More than 20 above-designated-size enterprises, 50+ high-tech enterprises, and around 80 innovative pharmaceutical/technology enterprises are claimed in different OCR passages; another duplicated English passage says “more than 60,” so the precise count is inconsistent in the source.
- Hundreds of products are said to have NMPA, FDA or CE certifications.
- The brochure projects 10+ companies to be cultivated for listing within five years.
- A stated full-buildout target is around **RMB 30 billion annual output**, around **RMB 3 billion tax revenue**, and **15,000 jobs**.

These are brochure claims and may describe different dates or targets. They should not be treated as independently confirmed current figures.

## Larger Liangjiang targets

The brochure says the life-and-health sector had become one of Liangjiang New Area's three pillar industries. It reports that in **2024** the sector's total scale was **RMB 13.11 billion**, with above-designated-size industrial output of **RMB 8.876 billion**, about 10% of Chongqing's total, and nearly 300 related companies in the area. A separate section describes earlier “over 200” R&D, production and technical-service enterprises, illustrating that figures differ by date and definition.

Targets in the brochure:
- **2025:** initially establish a recognisable life-and-health industrial cluster in western China and exceed RMB 50 billion in industry scale.
- **2035:** exceed RMB 100 billion, with 100+ pharmaceutical companies and 1,000+ medical-device companies.

The three themed parks have separate 2035 output targets: D-Park RMB 26 billion, M-Park RMB 40 billion, and the formulation-centre area RMB 20 billion. These are targets, not achieved results.

## What makes the park attractive to companies?

The brochure emphasises four linked supports:
- **Industrial park:** physical space and facilities.
- **Innovation incubator:** help for start-ups and early-stage projects.
- **Industrial fund:** investment and financing.
- **Industry alliance:** connections to partners, expertise, suppliers and customers.

It also describes shared technical platforms for testing, certification, medical-device engineering, biomedical R&D, clinical/medical translation, cold-chain transport, antibody development, bioanalysis and contract development/manufacturing.

In plain English: the park is selling more than rent. It is selling access to a network of infrastructure, expertise, capital, regulatory/testing capabilities and potential collaborators.

Company descriptions, market-leadership statements, patent counts, regulatory claims and clinical outcomes are promotional brochure claims and require independent due diligence before relying on them.

## What this means commercially

The opportunity is not only in inventing a medicine. The ecosystem also needs:
- testing and certification;
- medical-device design and manufacturing;
- regulatory and quality-system support;
- contract R&D and manufacturing;
- clinical research and data;
- lab equipment and specialist services;
- cold-chain logistics;
- digital-health software and AI;
- financing, commercialisation and international market access.

For a technology or services business, the key question is which of these needs local companies still struggle to solve, and whether the park offers an accessible route to buyers, partners, pilot sites or incentives.

## Due-diligence questions to ask

1. Which companies are physically operating in the park today, rather than merely listed in a brochure?
2. Which shared platforms are operational, what do they cost, and can an outside SME use them?
3. What grants, tax incentives, rent subsidies or R&D support are currently available, and what are the eligibility rules?
4. Which hospitals and research institutes will support pilots or clinical validation?
5. What approvals are needed to sell in China and overseas, and who helps with NMPA/FDA/CE processes?
6. What are the current occupancy, revenue, employment and output figures, and what year does each number refer to?
7. For international firms, what are the requirements around ownership, IP, data, local partnerships and repatriation of revenue?
